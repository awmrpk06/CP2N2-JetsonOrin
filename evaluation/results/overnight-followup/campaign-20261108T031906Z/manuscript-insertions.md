## Methods

We conducted three independent paired blocks at 50 offered requests/s, comparing the unchanged pinned v5.1.1 lifecycle store with the evaluation-layer full-history index, and three independent paired 30-minute blocks at 10 requests/s, comparing 250 ms and 2,000 ms sampling of the same process-tree and tegrastats monitoring stack. Every condition used a fresh worker and backend state, default Python GC, full lifecycle retention, the same periodic skip-expired-slots scheduler, request logging, and a release-relative completion deadline equal to one period including maintenance. Trials were sequential with at least 60 seconds between adjacent runs. Throughput is completed requests divided by worker active time; misses and skipped releases are reported with executed/planned denominators. Replication is at the run-pair level, not per request.

## Results

At 50 offered requests/s, indexed-minus-pinned paired differences were +3.733, +3.741, and +2.052 requests/s in H01-H03; cycle-median differences were -8.509, -7.206, and -7.305 ms, and cycle-p95 differences were -17.672, -17.392, and -15.135 ms. The direction was consistent across the three independent pairs. Nevertheless, every high-rate trial was performance-flagged: pinned runs had 5,193/5,092/4,012 deadline misses and 1,143/1,146/639 skipped releases (15,000 planned releases each), while indexed runs had 8/9/7 misses and 24 skipped releases each. The matched first-10,000-completed-request results are reported by pair in `high-rate-prefix-pairs.csv`; all six primary runs contributed exactly 10,000 requests. Their indexed-minus-pinned cycle-median differences were -7.126, -6.973, and -6.905 ms, and p95 differences were -10.624, -10.718, and -8.419 ms. The 1,000-request block detail, including sampled retained-history counts, is in `high-rate-prefix.csv`. Indexed runs used about 40% of one logical core for the worker versus about 77% for pinned runs.

Monitoring paired differences (2,000 ms minus 250 ms) were: M01, throughput -0.000 requests/s, cycle median +0.307 ms, p95 +0.089 ms; M02, -0.000 requests/s, +2.596 ms, +6.741 ms; and M03, +0.000 requests/s, -1.817 ms, -0.387 ms. Latency-difference direction was not consistent across the three pairs; the largest difference (M02) arises mainly from one 250 ms run whose latency grew unusually slowly. Every monitoring trial had 5 deadline misses among 17,999 executed requests and 1 skipped release among 18,000 planned releases; these misses occurred at the same completed-request positions in all six runs regardless of sampling interval. Observer CPU fell by 10.5-10.6 percentage points of one logical core at 2,000 ms (from about 12.7% to 2.2%).

Full per-run values, ranges and counts are in `results-summary.md`, `per-run-results.csv` and `paired-differences.csv`. These are descriptive results from three independent run-pairs per comparison; they do not establish equivalence or non-interference.

## Limitations

The monitoring treatment combines sampling frequency, serialization/logging and periodic resource-guard cadence; it is not a monitoring-disabled comparison. Two-second sampling can miss short CPU, memory and temperature peaks. The experiment uses one Jetson, a simulated edge backend, a limited duration and three independent pairs per question. Indexing retains full history, but findings are specific to this workload and do not establish general CP2N2 capacity. The 25 W mode is a configured platform mode, not measured power consumption; platform observations are not control-plane-only energy. Room temperature is an approximate user estimate, not a continuous measurement.

## Professor Comments 4 and 6

- **Comment 4 (monitoring overhead):** We ran three independent paired 30-minute blocks at 10 requests/s, sampling the same process-tree and tegrastats monitoring stack every 250 ms or every 2,000 ms, with unchanged guards. Reducing the sampling frequency lowered observer CPU from about 12.7% to 2.2% of one logical core in every pair, but produced no consistent latency change: 2,000 - 250 ms cycle-median differences were +0.31, +2.60 and -1.82 ms (p95 +0.09, +6.74 and -0.39 ms), throughput was 9.999 requests/s in all six runs, and misses/skips were identical (5/17,999 executed and 1/18,000 planned per run). With three pairs, we cannot exclude small effects or claim equivalence. This compares two settings of the combined monitoring configuration and does not measure unmonitored execution.
- **Comment 6 (high-rate indexed execution):** We ran three independent paired 5-minute blocks at 50 offered requests/s, comparing the unchanged pinned store with the full-retention index. Indexing improved every pair: throughput +3.73, +3.74 and +2.05 requests/s; cycle median -8.51, -7.21 and -7.31 ms; and cycle p95 -17.67, -17.39 and -15.14 ms. Deadline misses fell from 5,193/13,857, 5,092/13,854 and 4,012/14,361 executed requests to 8, 9 and 7 out of 14,976, and skipped releases fell from 1,143, 1,146 and 639 to 24 out of 15,000 planned. Over the first 10,000 completed requests, pinned block medians rose from 8.7-10.0 ms to 18.4-20.8 ms as retained history grew, whereas indexed medians remained at 8.3-9.9 ms (matched-prefix median differences -7.13, -6.97 and -6.91 ms). Indexed execution was not miss-free, still retains the full history, and had higher sampled worker RSS (about 200 versus 181-185 MiB). These results apply to this simulated workload and duration only and do not establish general CP2N2 or Jetson capacity.
